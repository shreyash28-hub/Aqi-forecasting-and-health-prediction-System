# Phase 2: interim observations and conclusions

Generated by `python -m src.phase2`. All numbers come from the tables in this folder.

## Data and preprocessing

- Six cities, 10,711 daily observations in total; gaps up to 21 days are time-interpolated (0.5-4.1% of days per city) and only the last segment after a longer gap is kept.
- Models are fitted on log(1 + AQI): AQI is right-skewed and its seasonal swing grows with the level, so the log makes the seasonality additive and the variance steadier. Forecasts are transformed back and scored on the AQI scale.
- Ahmedabad's AQI is recomputed without CO (faulty CO sensor), so its series is comparable to the others.

## Stationarity

- ADF rejects a unit root for log AQI in all 6 cities (p < 0.05), so d = 0 is used. KPSS rejects level-stationarity in 5 of them: the series is stationary around a slowly moving level (mainly the annual cycle), not around a constant. The first difference passes both tests everywhere.
- So d = 0 and the annual cycle is modelled explicitly instead of differenced away: SARIMA gets 3 pairs of annual Fourier terms (a period-365 seasonal ARIMA would need a 365-lag polynomial, too slow and unstable on ~1,900 days).

## Parameter selection

- **Delhi**: ARIMA(2, 0, 2); SARIMA(2, 0, 2)x(0, 0, 1, 7) + 3 annual Fourier pairs; Holt-Winters (add. damped trend, add. seasonal m=7).
- **Bengaluru**: ARIMA(2, 0, 1); SARIMA(2, 0, 1)x(0, 0, 1, 7) + 3 annual Fourier pairs; Holt-Winters (add. damped trend, add. seasonal m=7).
- **Chennai**: ARIMA(2, 0, 2); SARIMA(2, 0, 2)x(0, 0, 1, 7) + 3 annual Fourier pairs; Holt-Winters (add. damped trend, add. seasonal m=7).
- **Hyderabad**: ARIMA(3, 0, 1); SARIMA(2, 0, 2)x(0, 0, 1, 7) + 3 annual Fourier pairs; Holt-Winters (add. damped trend, add. seasonal m=7).
- **Lucknow**: ARIMA(2, 0, 2); SARIMA(2, 0, 2)x(0, 0, 1, 7) + 3 annual Fourier pairs; Holt-Winters (add. damped trend, add. seasonal m=7).
- **Ahmedabad**: ARIMA(2, 0, 2); SARIMA(2, 0, 2)x(0, 0, 1, 7) + 3 annual Fourier pairs; Holt-Winters (add. damped trend, add. seasonal m=7).
- ARIMA (p, q) is the AIC minimum over p, q ≤ 3; SARIMA keeps p, q ≤ 2 and picks the weekly (P, Q) by AIC; Holt-Winters picks the seasonal period (7 or 365) by AIC and estimates the smoothing parameters by maximum likelihood.
- Holt-Winters: AIC chose the weekly period over 365 in 6 of 6 cities (365 seasonal states cost more than they gain). The level smoothing alpha is high (0.46-0.94: tomorrow follows today closely), the trend smoothing is ~0 and the trend is damped (phi 0.80-0.99), so long-range forecasts flatten out.
- The weekly SARIMA term is small in every city (seasonal MA coefficient between -0.06 and 0.03): the weekday cycle in AQI is weak. SARIMA's gain over ARIMA comes from the annual Fourier terms.
- AIC compares candidates within one model family only; Holt-Winters' AIC is computed on a different basis and is not comparable with ARIMA's.

## Residual diagnostics

- ARIMA / SARIMA: Ljung-Box at lag 14 finds no remaining autocorrelation (p > 0.05) in 8 of 12 fits; the exceptions are Chennai, Hyderabad, where a little structure at lags 8-14 is left (with ~1,900 observations even small effects become significant).
- Holt-Winters fails Ljung-Box in all 6 cities (p < 0.001): exponential smoothing has no short-term autoregressive term, so day-to-day persistence stays in the residuals. Its point forecasts can still be competitive (it wins in two cities), but its prediction intervals should not be trusted.
- Residual means are ~0 everywhere (no bias). Residuals are heavy-tailed (Jarque-Bera rejects normality everywhere, kurtosis 4.5-13.0 vs 3 for a normal): pollution episodes such as Diwali and crop-burning days are sudden spikes the models cannot anticipate. This mainly makes normal-theory prediction intervals too narrow.

## Forecast accuracy (6 rolling 30-day windows)

- **Delhi**: SARIMA has the lowest mean RMSE (69.21, MAPE 26.2%), 30.0% below the best naive baseline (Naive (last value), 98.91); it beats every baseline in 4/6 windows.
- **Bengaluru**: SARIMA has the lowest mean RMSE (19.35, MAPE 20.0%), 24.3% below the best naive baseline (Seasonal naive (weekly), 25.55); it beats every baseline in 2/6 windows.
- **Chennai**: ARIMA has the lowest mean RMSE (37.45, MAPE 24.7%), 12.6% below the best naive baseline (Seasonal naive (weekly), 42.87); it beats every baseline in 4/6 windows.
- **Hyderabad**: Holt-Winters has the lowest mean RMSE (25.84, MAPE 33.8%), 0.2% below the best naive baseline (Naive (last value), 25.88); it beats every baseline in 2/6 windows.
- **Lucknow**: SARIMA has the lowest mean RMSE (60.01, MAPE 31.1%), 27.8% below the best naive baseline (Naive (last value), 83.16); it beats every baseline in 4/6 windows.
- **Ahmedabad**: Holt-Winters has the lowest mean RMSE (57.44, MAPE 32.2%), 5.4% below the best naive baseline (Naive (last value), 60.75); it beats every baseline in 4/6 windows.
- Overall: SARIMA wins in 3, Holt-Winters wins in 2, ARIMA wins in 1 of 6 cities. A classical model beats the naive baselines on mean RMSE in 6 of 6 cities.

## What this means for air-quality forecasting

- Errors are largest in Delhi and Lucknow, where winter AQI swings by hundreds of points, and smallest in Bengaluru and Hyderabad. A 30-day forecast gets the level and the season right but not individual spike days.
- SARIMA's weekly + annual structure helps most in cities with a strong winter peak; in a calm month a naive forecast is hard to beat, which is why the evaluation uses six windows across the year instead of one hold-out.
