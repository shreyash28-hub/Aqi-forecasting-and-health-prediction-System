Why Airware

The same air affects people differently.

A city's AQI tells you how polluted the air is, not what it means for you. Age, lung or heart conditions, time spent outdoors and mask use all change the risk. Airware combines the forecast with your profile.

Data and methodology

Built on official monitoring data.

Forecasts are trained on daily readings from Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) monitoring stations, and AQI follows the official CPCB National Air Quality Index.

Good0–50

Minimal impact.

Satisfactory51–100

Minor discomfort for sensitive people.

Moderate101–200

Discomfort for people with lung or heart disease.

Poor201–300

Discomfort for most people on long exposure.

Very poor301–400

Respiratory illness on prolonged exposure.

Severe401–500

Affects healthy people; serious for the sick.

Six major cities

Delhi, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Lucknow and Ahmedabad, chosen for the completeness of their monitoring records.

Cleaned and checked

Short gaps are filled, faulty sensor readings are corrected, and seasonality is tested before modelling.

Personal risk model

A neural network estimates risk from your profile and the forecast AQI, PM2.5 and NO2 for each day.

FAQ

Common questions

What is AQI?

The Air Quality Index turns pollutant levels into one number from 0 to 500. Airware uses India's official CPCB scale, from Good to Severe.

How accurate are the forecasts?

Each city's model was tested on six separate 30-day periods and beats simple baseline forecasts by 5–33% on average. Accuracy is highest for the next few days.

Is the health risk a medical diagnosis?

No. It's an estimate for planning and decision support. Always consult a healthcare professional for medical advice.

Do I need an account?

No, forecasts are free to view. An account lets you save your profile once and keep a history of your estimates.
